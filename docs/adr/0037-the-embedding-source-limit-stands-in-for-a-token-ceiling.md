# The embedding source limit is a character cap standing in for a token ceiling

`EMBEDDING_SOURCE_LIMIT` truncates the embedded text at 20,000 characters, to keep the embed call under Titan Text Embeddings V2's ceiling of 8,192 tokens. The two units do not convert, and that is the whole difficulty: no character count can guarantee a token count. The limit was 45,000 until this decision because somebody took the model's other published figure, 50,000 characters, and left a margin under it. That figure never binds, because the token ceiling is reached first. Nothing had hit it, because every project in the corpus is under 9,000 characters of source; the legacy corpus is what breaks that, with eleven rows from one sponsor, three of them at 34,000 to 36,000 characters of pasted links, refused at 9,999, 9,202 and 13,103 tokens when measured against Bedrock. 20,000 sits just under the project form's own ceiling, about 20,310 characters, being the seven field caps at 20,200 plus the labels and joins `buildProjectEmbeddingSource` adds, so the only proposal this truncates at all is one filled to the last field, which loses a few hundred characters of license text. Measured rather than extrapolated: the first 20,000 characters of the densest of those legacy rows, which is what the builder would actually send, tokenise to 7,712 against the ceiling of 8,192. That is 2.59 characters per token and a margin of about 6 percent. Taking the density of the untruncated row instead gives 2.63 and overstates the margin, because density is not uniform along a paste of links. The alternative, catching the rejection inside `bedrockEmbed` and retrying on a shorter input, was rejected: it embeds a truncated string while `embeddingHash` covers the full one, so the stored vector stops matching what its hash says it is and a later change to the retry ratio invalidates nothing. Truncating in the source builder keeps the hash over exactly what was embedded, which is the property the hash exists for. Decided 2026-09-20, with no issue behind it: this was found while measuring a held cohort of legacy rows against Bedrock, and the bug it fixes predates them by a year.

## Consequences

Changing this number re-embeds every row longer than the smaller of the old value and the new one, at one paid Bedrock call each, because the truncated string is what the hash covers. This change moves no stored hash today, for a reason narrower than the form's caps: the longest embedding source among the 906 rows already imported is 8,990 characters, and nothing written through the form comes near its own ceiling either. A row in the 310-character band between 20,000 and that ceiling would re-hash and re-embed once, which is the whole cost this could have. Text denser than 2.44 characters per token would still overflow 20,000 characters. The densest prefix measured is 2.59, across the five legacy rows tokenised, so nothing known trips it. Closing that gap is deliberately not attempted, and a row that did overflow would behave exactly as an oversized row does today: `refreshProjectEmbedding` returns `"failed"`, the vector stays null, the row sorts last under `recommended` (#427), and every later sweep retries it at one wasted call. `src/lib/__tests__/embedding-source.test.ts` pins the arithmetic the number was chosen by rather than the number itself, so the density assumption is visible instead of folded into a literal; it is a floor on the reasoning, not on the limit, and it admits any value under about 21,240. What fails on a change to the number itself is the literal in `src/test/backfill-embeddings-parity.test.ts`, which pins it across the three places it lives: the lib, the `.mjs` the production image ships (ADR-0024), and the test's own copy.
