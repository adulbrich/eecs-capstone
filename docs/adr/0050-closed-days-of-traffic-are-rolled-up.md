# Closed days of traffic are rolled up into visits

Amends [ADR-0048](./0048-a-visitor-is-a-daily-salted-hash.md), which said visits are derived at query time and never stored. The derivation is unchanged, but `/admin/traffic` (#592) now reads a closed day's visits from `traffic_visits`, one row per visit, filled from `traffic_events` by the same query that derives today's. Deriving visits needs two ordered window passes over every event in the range, and at a million events a 90-day range took over 500 ms on every load against a 200 ms budget; indexes took the sorts away but not the passes, and the timings are on #592. A day's events never change once the day has closed, so its visits are computed once, on the first page load after local midnight plus a two-minute settle margin, under a transaction-scoped advisory lock that makes concurrent loads roll each day up once. #508 anticipated this as the fallback, and the two objections it raised against storing visits do not apply: the public endpoint is still one insert and never reads, since only a staff page load writes the rollup; and the 30 minutes is not baked into history, since `traffic_visits` is derived data that can be truncated at any time, and the next load rebuilds every day under whatever definition then holds. A rollup row keeps no visitor hash, no address and nothing about a person: the day, the entry page and its referring host, the country, browser and device, the pages viewed with their counts, the listing filters set, and whether it was that visitor's first visit of the day, which is how distinct visitors per day survive without the hash. The office's local date is a generated `day` column on `traffic_events`, spelled as a literal zone because a generated column takes no parameter, and a test holds it to `OFFICE_TIME_ZONE`. Decided and built in #592.
