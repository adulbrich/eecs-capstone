# Sign-in limits are sized for a shared address, and brute force moves elsewhere

Better Auth's rate limiter keys every bucket on the resolved viewer address and the path, and `createRateLimitKey(ip, path)` takes no configuration, so a rule can change how much an address may spend but never what an address means. OSU wireless NATs students into a pool of shared public addresses, which makes one bucket cover however many devices sit behind one pool address, so every number Better Auth ships is being asked a question it was not sized for. Its first special rule, 3 per 10 seconds, matches `/sign-in`, `/sign-up`, `/change-password` and `/change-email` as prefixes, and four of the five paths that reaches here check no credential at all: `/sign-in/oauth2` and `/sign-in/social` mint an OAuth state and redirect, so the password is typed at Microsoft or GitHub, who run their own defences, and `/sign-up/email` creates an account. Behind a pool address that rule is not brute force protection, it is a lockout for the fourth student in ten seconds on the two paths a new student reaches first, caused by nobody's behaviour and impossible to reproduce. So those four go to 60 per 10 seconds, which is past a lecture hall clicking at once and short of a script, and `/change-password` goes to 20 rather than 60 because it does verify the current password and so remains a real surface for whoever holds a stolen session. `/get-session` stops being limited at all, because `authClient.useSession()` calls it on every page render, 136 of the 139 `/api/auth/*` requests measured in production, and it reads a session the caller already holds a cookie for. `/sign-in/oauth2` keeps a limit rather than being switched off only because Better Auth refetches the ONID discovery document from Microsoft on every call, uncached (#553), and an unlimited path there is an amplifier pointed at the university's identity provider. The consequence worth stating plainly is that this repo now has no brute force protection on `/sign-in/email` beyond a number chosen to avoid locking out a lecture hall, and that is deliberate rather than overlooked: per-address limiting cannot protect a credential behind NAT at any number, so the control moves to a per-account attempt counter keyed on the account and the address together (#552). Decided 2026-09-21 in #535.

## Consequences

The numbers are honest about what they are, which is a cap on scripted volume, and nothing in the codebase should be read as claiming more until #552 lands. Until it does, the window is real: an attacker gets 60 guesses per 10 seconds per address against a known account, where before they got 3, and the mitigating facts are that they already had roughly five times the stated budget through CloudFront edge dilution, and that the same dilution is what the other half of #535 removes. Shipping the two halves in the wrong order would make campus worse, which is why the limits deploy before the load balancer changes. Raising `/sign-in/email` also raised the ceiling on the verification mail that a refused sign-in sends, from 3 to 60 per 10 seconds per address; reaching it needs the correct password for an unverified account, so the only inbox anyone can flood is one they already control, and the note sits on `emailVerification.sendOnSignIn` where someone changing that flag will find it. The per-task in-memory counter is untouched and still multiplies every number by the running task count, between two and four under the autoscaling from #529: that loosens rather than tightens, so it is a known overshoot rather than a hole, and `storage: "database"` was rejected here because once `/get-session` is exempt the remaining limited paths are three requests in 139 and not worth a table and a write per request. The rejected alternatives for `/sign-in/email` were Cloudflare Turnstile and AWS WAF account takeover prevention; both work, and both were declined in favour of #552 because they add a vendor, and because a captcha fails closed on a missing token, so it would break every browser test that signs in through the password form unless gated to production, which means shipping a control CI never runs.
