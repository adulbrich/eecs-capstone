# A visitor is a salted hash that cannot outlive its day

The traffic writer identifies a visitor by SHA-256 over a daily salt, the viewer address (an IPv6 address cut to its /64), the browser family and major version, the OS family and the device class, truncated to 16 bytes; the raw address and user agent are never stored or logged. The salt is 32 random bytes in a one-row `UNLOGGED` `traffic_salt` table, replaced on the first event of each local day and never archived, and not derived from an application secret, because an overwritten random salt makes yesterday's hashes unlinkable to anyone, us included. Being unlogged keeps it out of backups, and a crash or failover splits visitors for the rest of that day. A visit is 30 minutes without an event, ended by the salt day; ADR-0050 stores closed days. `/admin/traffic` states what this gives up: visitors cannot be summed across days, campus NAT collapses students into few visitors, and a visit across midnight counts twice. Decided in #508.
