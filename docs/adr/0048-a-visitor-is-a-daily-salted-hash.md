# A visitor is a salted hash that cannot outlive its day

The traffic writer (#591) identifies a visitor by SHA-256 over a daily salt, the viewer address, the browser family and major version, the OS family and the device class, truncated to 16 bytes. The raw address and the raw user agent are never stored or logged, and an IPv6 address is reduced to its /64 before hashing, the part a household keeps rather than the part a device rotates. The salt is 32 random bytes in a one-row `traffic_salt` table, replaced by `TRUNCATE` then `INSERT` on the first event of each local day in `OFFICE_TIME_ZONE` and never archived; it is not derived from an application secret, because a derived salt can be recomputed forever by anyone holding the secret, while an overwritten random one makes yesterday's hashes unlinkable to anyone, us included, and that is the sentence the privacy page makes. The table is `UNLOGGED`, which Drizzle cannot declare, so the migration says it by hand: Postgres keeps it out of the WAL, RDS backups and point-in-time restores bring it back empty, and a truncate leaves no dead tuple. The accepted cost is that a crash or failover empties it mid-day and visitors split for the rest of that day. A visit, 30 minutes without an event and ended by the salt day regardless, is derived at query time and never stored, because storing one turns the public endpoint from one insert into a read then a write and bakes the 30 minutes into history. What this gives up, each to be stated on `/admin/traffic` (#592): visitors are per day and cannot be summed across days, students behind campus NAT collapse into few visitors, a visit across local midnight counts twice, and an iPad reads as a Mac. The raw user agent is not an input, because its entropy is uneven across browsers and a rotating scraper would become hundreds of visitors. Decided in #508; built in #591.
