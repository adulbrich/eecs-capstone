# Access logs keep raw addresses, and the retention rule is the control

ALB and CloudFront access logging are on, writing to one private encrypted bucket with a lifecycle rule that deletes objects after 30 days. Both formats record the full client IP of every request, which is the opposite of what #508 chose for the traffic writer, where the address is hashed and the raw value is never stored. The two are not in conflict and the difference is worth stating so nobody reads one as a precedent for the other: the traffic writer's purpose is a long-lived analytics table that anyone on staff may read, so the address is not needed and keeping it would be gratuitous, while an access log's whole purpose is to answer "which request, from where" during an incident, and a hashed address answers nothing. The control on the operational side is therefore retention rather than collection. Thirty days is chosen to match the existing CloudWatch log group so there is one number to remember, and the lifecycle rule expires rather than transitions to cheaper storage, because a visitor address in Glacier is still a visitor address. What this buys is the class of question that was unanswerable before it: #519 could confirm a misconfiguration from a warning line but never how many students it actually refused, and the 2026-09-17 spike is permanently unknowable. CloudFront uses standard logging v2 rather than the `logging_config` block on the distribution, which is the obvious thing to reach for: the older path requires the destination bucket to enable ACLs and grant the `awslogsdelivery` account full control, and turning `BucketOwnerEnforced` off on a bucket holding visitor addresses to save two resources is a bad trade. Decided 2026-09-20 in #523.

## Consequences

Cost is about 20 cents a month at today's volume, which is 180,664 ALB requests and 174,824 CloudFront requests over 30 days: roughly 250 MB, most of it the vended-log delivery charge rather than storage. The assets distribution gets no logs, because it serves content-hashed files from S3 and answers no question anyone has asked; adding it later is one delivery source. Vended log delivery chooses its own key prefix, `AWSLogs/<account-id>/CloudFront/`, and a `suffix_path` appends to that rather than replacing it, so the bucket policy is scoped by principal and source account instead of by prefix: a policy naming the wrong prefix does not fail loudly, it just leaves the bucket empty. `/privacy` says nothing about these logs today, and it should, but the paragraph belongs to #513, which is already writing the traffic writer's; this change tells that issue the logs exist rather than writing a second paragraph that would conflict with it. The retention number is a variable rather than a literal so that raising it for an incident is a one-line apply, and anyone raising it is changing how long visitor addresses are kept, which is the thing to think about rather than the number. Whether these logs are ever read is the open question: nothing queries them, and the first person who needs to will reach for Athena over the bucket, which nothing sets up here.
